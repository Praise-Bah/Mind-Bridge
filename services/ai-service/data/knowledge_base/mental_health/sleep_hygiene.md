# Sleep Hygiene: Better Sleep for Better Mental Health

## Why Sleep Matters

Sleep is not a luxury — it is a biological necessity. Your brain uses sleep to consolidate memories, process emotions, repair cells, and clear metabolic waste. For university students, sleep quality directly impacts academic performance, emotional resilience, and mental health.

Research consistently shows that:
- Students who sleep 7-9 hours per night have significantly better GPAs than those who sleep less
- Sleep deprivation increases the risk of anxiety and depression by 2-3 times
- Even one night of poor sleep impairs concentration, memory, and decision-making to a level comparable to mild alcohol intoxication
- Chronic sleep deprivation weakens the immune system, making you more susceptible to illness

Despite this, university students are one of the most sleep-deprived populations. Late-night studying, social activities, screen use, and irregular schedules all contribute to poor sleep.

## Understanding Sleep

### Sleep Cycles
A typical night's sleep consists of 4-6 cycles, each lasting about 90 minutes. Each cycle includes:
- **Light sleep (Stages 1-2):** Your body begins to relax. Easy to wake from.
- **Deep sleep (Stage 3):** Critical for physical restoration and memory consolidation. Very difficult to wake from.
- **REM sleep:** Where most dreaming occurs. Essential for emotional processing, creativity, and learning.

Disrupting these cycles — even if you are in bed for 8 hours — reduces sleep quality. This is why consistency matters as much as duration.

### How Much Sleep Do You Need?
Young adults (18-25) need 7-9 hours of sleep per night. Individual needs vary, but very few people can function well on less than 7 hours consistently.

## Sleep Hygiene Tips

Sleep hygiene refers to the habits and practices that promote consistent, restorative sleep.

### 1. Keep a Consistent Schedule
- Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, including weekends.
- Sleeping in on weekends disrupts your circadian rhythm and makes Monday mornings harder.
- If you need to adjust your schedule, shift it by no more than 30 minutes per day.

### 2. Create a Sleep-Friendly Environment
- **Darkness:** Use curtains or a sleep mask. Light suppresses melatonin production.
- **Cool temperature:** A slightly cool room (around 18-20 degrees Celsius) is optimal for sleep.
- **Quiet:** Use earplugs if your environment is noisy.
- **Comfortable bedding:** Invest in a pillow and bedding that support good sleep posture.
- **Reserve your bed for sleep:** Avoid studying, eating, or scrolling through your phone in bed. This trains your brain to associate bed with sleep.

### 3. Manage Screen Time
- Blue light from phones, laptops, and tablets suppresses melatonin and tricks your brain into thinking it is daytime.
- Stop using screens at least 30-60 minutes before bed.
- If you must use a device, enable night mode or blue light filtering.
- Avoid stimulating content (social media arguments, exciting videos, stressful news) close to bedtime.

### 4. Watch What You Consume
- **Caffeine:** Avoid caffeine (coffee, tea, energy drinks, chocolate) at least 6 hours before bed. Caffeine has a half-life of 5-6 hours, meaning half of it is still in your system hours after you drink it.
- **Alcohol:** While alcohol may make you feel sleepy, it severely disrupts sleep quality, reducing REM sleep and causing nighttime awakenings.
- **Heavy meals:** Avoid large meals within 2-3 hours of bedtime. A light snack is fine.
- **Water:** Stay hydrated during the day but reduce fluid intake close to bedtime to avoid nighttime bathroom trips.

### 5. Build a Wind-Down Routine
A consistent pre-sleep routine signals to your body that it is time to sleep. Spend 30-60 minutes before bed doing calming activities:
- Reading (not on a screen)
- Gentle stretching or yoga
- Listening to calm music or a podcast
- Journaling or writing down tomorrow's to-do list (this can reduce racing thoughts)
- Progressive muscle relaxation or a body scan meditation
- Taking a warm shower (the subsequent cooling of your body promotes sleepiness)

### 6. Manage Naps Wisely
- If you nap, keep it under 20-30 minutes and before 3:00 PM.
- Long or late naps interfere with nighttime sleep.
- A short "power nap" can boost alertness and performance without disrupting your sleep schedule.

## Managing Insomnia

If you regularly have trouble falling or staying asleep, you may be experiencing insomnia. Here are evidence-based strategies:

### Stimulus Control
- Go to bed only when you feel sleepy.
- If you cannot fall asleep within 20 minutes, get up and do something quiet and boring in dim light (not your phone). Return to bed when you feel sleepy.
- This retrains your brain to associate bed with falling asleep quickly.

### Cognitive Strategies for Racing Thoughts
- **The worry journal:** Before bed, write down everything on your mind. Tell yourself you have "captured" these thoughts and can address them tomorrow.
- **Counting technique:** Slowly count backwards from 300 by 3s. This occupies the cognitive mind just enough to prevent worry spirals.
- **Body scan meditation:** Systematically relaxing each body part redirects attention from thoughts to physical sensations.

### What to Avoid
- Do not watch the clock. Turn it away from you. Clock-watching increases anxiety about not sleeping.
- Do not try harder to sleep. The effort to fall asleep creates arousal that prevents sleep. Instead, focus on simply resting.
- Do not use sleep as an escape from stress. Address stress through proper coping strategies during the day.

## When to Seek Help

Consult a health professional if:
- You consistently take more than 30 minutes to fall asleep
- You wake up multiple times per night and cannot get back to sleep
- You feel unrefreshed despite adequate time in bed
- Sleepiness interferes with your daily functioning
- You experience regular nightmares or night terrors
- You suspect you have sleep apnea (loud snoring, gasping during sleep)

Good sleep is one of the foundations of mental health. Improving your sleep habits can have a positive ripple effect on your mood, energy, focus, and overall wellbeing. MindBridge encourages you to make sleep a priority — your brain will thank you.
