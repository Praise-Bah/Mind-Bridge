# Crisis Resources and Emergency Help

## If You Are in Immediate Danger

If you or someone you know is in immediate physical danger, call emergency services right away:
- **Cameroon Emergency Number:** 112 (general emergency)
- **Police:** 117
- **Fire/Ambulance:** 118

**If someone has taken an overdose, is seriously injured, or is in immediate danger of harming themselves or others, do not wait — call emergency services immediately.**

## Crisis Hotlines

### Cameroon
- **SOS Aide Cameroon:** 1510 (toll-free crisis hotline providing immediate emotional support and referrals)

### International Resources (accessible from Cameroon)
- **Crisis Text Line:** Text "HELLO" to 741741 (available via SMS where service is supported)
- **Befrienders Worldwide:** www.befrienders.org (directory of crisis centers by country)
- **International Association for Suicide Prevention:** https://www.iasp.info/resources/Crisis_Centres/

### Online Support
- **7 Cups:** www.7cups.com (free online chat with trained listeners, available 24/7)
- **MindBridge Chat:** Available through the MindBridge platform for students at ICT University

## Recognizing a Mental Health Crisis

A mental health crisis is any situation in which a person's behavior puts them at risk of hurting themselves or others, or prevents them from being able to care for themselves or function in daily life.

### Warning Signs of a Crisis
- Talking about wanting to die, kill oneself, or having no reason to live
- Looking for ways to harm oneself (searching online, seeking access to means)
- Talking about feeling trapped, hopeless, or being a burden to others
- Increasing use of alcohol or drugs
- Acting recklessly or engaging in risky activities
- Withdrawing from friends, family, and activities
- Extreme mood swings — from very high to very low
- Giving away prized possessions
- Saying goodbye to people as if it is the last time
- Expressing unbearable emotional pain
- Sudden calmness after a period of depression (this can indicate a decision has been made)

## What to Do If You Are in Crisis

### If You Are Having Thoughts of Suicide
1. **Tell someone immediately.** Call a crisis hotline (SOS Aide Cameroon: 1510), a trusted friend, family member, or go to the nearest emergency room.
2. **Remove access to means of harm.** If you have access to medications, sharp objects, or other means of self-harm, put distance between yourself and these items. Give them to someone else if possible.
3. **Do not be alone.** Go to a public place or stay with someone you trust.
4. **Remember: These feelings are temporary.** Even when it does not feel like it, suicidal crises are time-limited. Most people who survive a suicidal crisis go on to live meaningful lives.
5. **You are not a burden.** Reaching out for help is the bravest thing you can do.

### If You Are Having a Panic Attack
1. Remind yourself: "This is a panic attack. It is uncomfortable but not dangerous. It will pass."
2. Focus on slow, controlled breathing: Inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale for 4.
3. Use the 5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique (5 things you see, 4 you feel, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste).
4. Place your feet flat on the ground and focus on the physical sensation of contact.
5. Most panic attacks peak within 10 minutes and subside within 20-30 minutes.

### If You Are Experiencing Severe Emotional Distress
1. Call someone you trust or a crisis hotline.
2. Remove yourself from any triggering situation if possible.
3. Use grounding and breathing techniques.
4. Avoid making major decisions while in acute distress.
5. Focus on getting through the next hour, the next minute if needed.

## Safety Planning

A safety plan is a personalized, practical plan you create in advance for times of crisis. Having a plan ready means you do not have to figure out what to do when you are in the middle of a crisis.

### How to Create a Safety Plan

**Step 1: Warning Signs**
List your personal warning signs that a crisis may be developing. These might include specific thoughts ("no one cares"), feelings (numbness, overwhelming despair), behaviors (isolating, not eating), or situations (after a conflict, during exams).

**Step 2: Internal Coping Strategies**
List things you can do on your own to cope: breathing exercises, physical activity, journaling, listening to music, prayer, taking a shower.

**Step 3: Social Settings and People for Distraction**
List people you can contact or places you can go to take your mind off distress: a friend's room, a busy campus area, a family member to call.

**Step 4: People to Ask for Help**
List 2-3 specific people you can reach out to for help during a crisis, with their phone numbers.

**Step 5: Professional Contacts**
List professional resources:
- SOS Aide Cameroon: 1510
- Your university counseling service
- Your doctor or therapist if you have one
- Nearest hospital emergency department

**Step 6: Making Your Environment Safe**
List steps to reduce access to means of harm: removing medications from your room, giving sharp objects to a trusted person, avoiding alcohol during vulnerable periods.

## How to Help Someone in Crisis

If you are worried about a friend or classmate:

1. **Ask directly.** Asking someone if they are thinking about suicide does not plant the idea — it opens the door for them to talk. "Are you thinking about hurting yourself?" is a brave and caring question.
2. **Listen without judgment.** Do not minimize their feelings, offer quick solutions, or say "it could be worse." Just listen.
3. **Take it seriously.** Even if you are not sure how serious it is, treat every expression of suicidal thoughts with care.
4. **Stay with them.** Do not leave someone alone if you believe they are at risk.
5. **Help them connect with professional support.** Offer to call a crisis line together or accompany them to counseling services.
6. **Follow up.** Check in with them in the days and weeks after a crisis. Ongoing support matters.

**You do not need to be a therapist to help someone.** Your presence, compassion, and willingness to listen can save a life.

## Remember

- Asking for help is a sign of strength, not weakness.
- Crisis feelings are temporary, even when they feel permanent.
- You matter, and your life has value.
- MindBridge is here to support you at every step.
